# Agent Note: Fantasy report weekly moves

Status: implemented

English | [中文](2026-10-19-fantasy-report-weekly-moves.zh.md)

## Problem

A manual research session for one team found moves the weekly report could not: a waiver claim on a player who was on waivers rather than a free agent, two Out players who could move into empty IR slots to free roster spots, a two-quarterback bye the next week and a defense bye the week after, a kicker swap that had to happen before a Thursday kickoff, receivers whose last two games far outran their projections while a famous name trailed his, consensus waiver articles, teammate injuries that opened a role, betting-line implied totals, target and snap share, and a quarterback surplus worth trading. The report read free agents only (`status=FA`), judged one week at a time, gave candidates no news, and fired on Wednesday afternoon, after Yahoo had already processed claims.

## Decision

Code owns every move that arithmetic decides, and the model keeps the same five stage kinds in the same order. The report adds an optional `waivers` mode, scheduled by `schedule.waivers` and requestable as `/fantasy-report <team> waivers`, for Tuesday morning before claims clear; without that schedule there is no waiver timer and no waiver catch-up slot.

- **Horizon.** The report week and the following weeks up to `horizonWeeks` (default 3) and `lastWeek`. Later weeks keep IR, PUP, NFI, SUSP, and NA statuses but drop Q, D, O, and GTD, which describe only the next game, and drop slot locks; each horizon week has its own projection call per player list.
- **Room and IR.** Unlocked players whose Yahoo positions include `IR` fill empty IR slots, and the freed spots count as open roster room. IR-slotted and moved players leave the horizon rosters.
- **Value.** The waiver pool reads Yahoo `status=A` at every starting position, the season rank list plus the top scorers of the last `waiverPointWeeks` weeks, `waiverPoolSize` players each, so claims and free agents arrive together and keep Yahoo's ownership type. A candidate's net value is the best-lineup total with him added, and on a full roster the cheapest droppable player removed, minus the current best-lineup total, averaged over the horizon, using the existing `lineup.ts` search. Equally cheap drops prefer the candidate's own position, so a better kicker or defense pairs with the one it replaces. The `waiverPositions` positions with the best net values keep `waiverCandidates` candidates each; `waiverPositions: 0` skips the pool, its moves, and the waiver stage. Picks pair with distinct drops in pick order, a pick no spot or worthwhile drop can take is left out with a caveat, and a claim names the best free agent at its position as its fallback.
- **Signals.** Exact-player Yahoo reads (`playerKeys`, 25 per read) give actual points for the last `formWeeks` weeks, flagged hot or cold against the projection by `formGapPoints`. An optional `ctx.fantasyNfl` gives each player's opponent, kickoff, and implied team total and recent usage; the plugin reads it through `ctx.get`, because the vendored Cordis declares no optional injection, and names its absence or failure in the caveats. The candidate news search covers the `candidateSearches` best candidates, and the waiver-article sweep admits up to `waiverArticlePages` pages that name an available player and counts distinct pages per player by normalized, word-bounded name.
- **Report.** A numbered checklist leads, each item with its deadline: lineup changes before the player's kickoff with Thursday and other early games called out, IR moves, claims before Yahoo processes them, adds and swaps, rechecks of questionable starters, and the final lineup before the first Sunday kickoff. Moves, the bye outlook, and the trade chip follow the lineup. Thursday and Sunday updates compare Yahoo statuses, the roster, and suggested moves with the latest earlier report of the same week, read from that run's evidence file through the attachment store; an unreadable file leaves the section out with a caveat. The delivery bound sheds bench reasons, then the bye outlook and trade chip, before cutting.

The waiver stage still answers `{"picks":[{"id","reason"}]}`; its data now carries claim or free agent, per-week projections, net value, the suggested drop, recent points and form, usage, the game and implied total, article mentions, and excerpts with source numbers, and its instructions weigh recent role and consensus over stale projections and forbid invented facts. Player-call sheets carry the same signals. The prompt version is `fantasy-weekly-v7`. Everything new lands in the evidence file.

## Alternatives considered

- **Let the model rank waiver candidates from raw lists** — rejected because marginal lineup value, cheapest drops, and IR room are exact computations over data code already holds; the model's contribution is weighing role news and consensus that projections miss, which the waiver stage still does.
- **Read only free agents and treat claims as unavailable** — rejected because the best pickups of a week are often on waivers, and an immediate free-agent fallback covers a failed claim.
- **Apply Yahoo injury statuses to every horizon week** — rejected because Q, D, and O describe only the next game; carrying them would undervalue players returning next week, while IR-type statuses do describe later weeks.
- **Value adds jointly as a set** — deferred; picks are few, and valuing each against the current roster with distinct drops keeps the search small and the reasoning legible.
- **Make the NFL data service a required injection** — rejected because a report without schedules is still useful; a missing service degrades with a caveat instead of failing the plugin load.
- **Rebuild the earlier report's facts from its Markdown** — rejected because the evidence file holds the exact roster, statuses, and moves; Markdown parsing would drift with every rendering change.

## Consequences

Reports now name claims, IR moves, byes, and early kickoffs before the deadlines that matter, and a Tuesday waiver report can run before claims clear. Each report makes more reads: one projection call per player list per horizon week, up to 3 × 6 waiver-pool lists, exact-player reads for recent points, and one NFL schedule and usage read; the waiver pool's values cost a few thousand small lineup searches. Without projections no candidate has net value, so the waiver stage does not run, unlike the earlier need-only shortlist. The [hybrid pipeline](2026-09-30-fantasy-report-hybrid-pipeline.md) still owns the stage split and per-item validation; its free-agent shortlist at weak positions is replaced by the net-value shortlist described here. The snapshot `snapshots/session/fantasy-report` replays the same recorded stage answers over richer fixtures: a claim with a free-agent fallback, two IR moves, a next-week defense bye, a Thursday game, and a waiver article.
